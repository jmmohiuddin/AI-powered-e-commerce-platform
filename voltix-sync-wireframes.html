Section 01

How to read this document

These are wireframes, not designs. They fix what is on each screen, in what order of importance, and how it behaves. They deliberately say nothing about typeface, colour, illustration or brand — those decisions come next, and they come easier once structure is settled.

Every screen carries the doubt it exists to answer, taken from the Product Design Document. A screen that does not answer a doubt should not be built. Numbered markers 1 on each frame correspond to the annotations beside it.

Conventions

Hatched blockImage, chart or content whose exact form is not yet decided.
Solid dark buttonThe single primary action. One per screen, no exceptions.
Outlined buttonSecondary. Present but not competing for attention.
Monospaced textSystem-generated: identifiers, quantities, timestamps.
● Coloured dotChannel or sync status. Always paired with a word — never colour alone.
Phone frame375 px. The default target. Desktop is the enhancement.

Annotations
  1. The headline is a sentence, not a gauge. “All channels in sync”, or “noon is 12 minutes behind”. A phrase can be read while walking; a dial has to be interpreted, and interpretation is slower than reading.
  2. Each row shows last confirmed write, not last attempt. “18 s ago” means a channel acknowledged it applied our number. An attempted write that was never confirmed is not progress and must not read like it.
  3. p95 is on the row, permanently. The product’s whole positioning rests on this figure being measured and published, which is why the word “real-time” appears nowhere in the interface.
  4. “Held back on purpose” is a section, not a footnote. Buffer and last-unit suppression are deliberate conservatism; if hidden, a listing showing fewer units than the warehouse holds looks like a bug. Tapping lists the SKUs and the reason for each.
  5. Oversells over 30 days stays on screen at zero. Especially at zero — it is the product’s report card and the answer to the doubt in one number.
  6. No green wash on the healthy state. Colour is spent sparingly so that red still means something when it appears.

Annotations
  1. “buffer basis” on the p99 row is the most important label in the product. It closes the loop between a measured number and the units being withheld, and it removes most of the “why is my listing showing less than I have?” confusion before it starts.
  2. The activity feed is the trust instrument. Watching real quantities change, in real time, with confirmation ticks, is the fastest route to believing a sync engine works. Deliberately verbose in the first weeks; it can be quietened later.
  3. Pause sits top-right, always reachable. The kill switch is not buried in settings. It must be findable in a panic, one-handed.
  4. Sparkline has no axis. Shape over time is the signal; precise values live in the rows above it.

Annotations
  1. Four glyphs in total: confirmed, held back, not listed, drift. Each additional symbol costs legibility, and this table gets scanned rather than read.
  2. Read SKU-2210: two available, noon shows two, Amazon shows zero with a held-back marker. Tapping it explains: “Last-unit rule — 2 units left, published to noon only (higher priority).” A deliberate decision, legible as one.
  3. Unmapped is the default filter after any bulk import. An unmapped SKU is silent and dangerous; it is the one condition that hides rather than announces itself.
  4. Bulk mapping is a review list, never auto-applied. Suggestions come from GTIN and SKU equality, and each row is accepted or rejected. A wrong mapping publishes one product’s stock to another product’s listing — the worst failure this product can produce.
  5. Unbuilt sections stay greyed in the sidebar rather than disappearing. An inherited decision from the existing admin, and a good one: it communicates roadmap without shipping dead links.
  6. Below 900 px this table becomes cards, not a horizontal scroll. A sideways table on a phone hides exactly the column you need and gives no sign it exists.

Annotations
  1. “Will publish to” is the most valuable component in the system. It shows the consequence of an action before the action is taken, per channel, with the buffer visible. It answers the doubt pre-emptively and teaches the buffer concept without a help article.
  2. The stepper is oversized and centred. Adjustment happens in a stockroom, often one-handed, often while holding a box.
  3. Reason code is mandatory. It writes to the append-only movement ledger and is what makes a variance investigable three weeks later. Options are short and concrete: goods receipt, damage, loss, found, return to supplier, correction, stocktake.
  4. Confirmation ticks appear one channel at a time, live. Those few seconds are the product proving itself. Do not optimise the animation away — it is the moment the merchant stops checking manually.
  5. Amazon lags noon visibly, and that is correct. The interface should not pretend two channels with different propagation characteristics behave identically.

Annotations

  4. Unit counts are shown for locked locations too. The merchant still needs to know how much stock sits at noon — he just cannot sell it anywhere else.

Annotations
  1. Excluded from v1, each because it breaks the budget: customer capture, discounts, multi-line baskets, split payment, printed receipts. Multi-line is the most likely to be requested and the most dangerous to add — it turns a single-decision screen into a basket-management screen. If it becomes necessary it belongs in a separate mode, entered deliberately.
  2. A sale at zero stock is allowed, not blocked. A physical item in a customer’s hand outranks a database row. It completes with a flagged negative adjustment and appears in the drift register. Staff who are blocked once stop using the tool entirely, and then every other screen in this document becomes a well-designed liar.
  3. “Syncing to 3 channels” on the success screen is written for the owner, who will look over a shoulder in the first fortnight. Four words, and it is the whole product.
  4. The offline banner states the accuracy caveat plainly. Selling continues; pretending the number is live when it is not would be worse than the outage.
  5. Touch targets are 56 px in this mode, not 44. The user is standing, hurried, and may be holding a product in the other hand.
  6. The whole flow is completable by scanner alone — scan, Enter, Enter. A barcode scanner is a keyboard, which makes the existing unverified focus-visible styling a blocker for this screen, not a nice-to-have.

Annotations

Annotations
  1. Each record reads as a sentence. Timestamp, channel, SKU, expected, actual, detection method, correction time and probable cause, written so it can be understood without training.
  2. An oversell record additionally names the money: the referral fee times price for noon, plus the account-health impact for Amazon. Naming the cost turns a technical event into a business fact.
  3. The register exists as evidence. Its readers are deciding whether the system can be trusted enough to stop double-checking by hand, so every entry is written to support or undermine that judgement honestly.
  4. The empty state carries the best news the product has. “No drift in 30 days” should be given the same weight on screen as a failure would be.

Motion

Motion has exactly one job here: showing that something is propagating. The channel confirmation tick is a 200 ms fade-and-scale and is the only moment of delight in the product. Activity-feed insertion is a 150 ms slide. Counter success is 250 ms then auto-dismiss. Everything else is static. All of it respects prefers-reduced-motion, degrading to instant state changes with no loss of information.

Section 06

Before visual design begins
Blocking

Three blocking questions, all on the counter flow. Every one changes the wireframe, so answer them before drawing anything at high fidelity.

DQ-01 — Is the counter a shared device with a shared login, or per-staff? Determines whether a sale is attributable, and whether a login step fits inside the 20-second budget.
DQ-02 — Which scanner hardware? A keyboard-wedge device and a camera-based scan are different components with different layouts.
DQ-03 — Confirm that a sale at zero stock completes with a flag rather than being blocked. These wireframes assume it does.

Per-screen checklist

  • All eight states specified: loading, empty, partial, error, offline, degraded, stale, success
  • Usable at 375 px, one-handed, primary action inside the thumb zone
  • Exactly one primary action
  • Every computed number has an Explainer
  • Every channel-derived value has a FreshnessStamp
  • Nothing renders as 0 where the truth is unknown — use an em dash
  • Colour is never the sole carrier of meaning; glyph and word accompany every status
  • Completable by keyboard alone, with visible focus
  • Mirrored and checked in RTL — these wireframes are all LTR
  • Copy avoids “real-time”, states what was queued rather than lost, and says what happens next
  • Skeleton preserves final layout with no shift on load

Not wireframed in v1

W-04 product create/edit, W-06 stocktake and W-11 order detail are specified in the Product Design Document but drawn only as structure notes here, because each is a conventional form or detail view that follows patterns already present in the admin. They should be wireframed alongside visual design rather than ahead of it. Payout reconciliation is P1 and intentionally excluded.
